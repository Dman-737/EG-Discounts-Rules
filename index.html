1. Purpose & Scope

This document defines the complete architecture for how discount codes and automated fees behave within ExpoGenie — from how organizers configure them, to how they display on exhibitor-facing invoices, to how every line item maps to a QuickBooks Product/Service record. The driving requirement is that product-level revenue must always be preserved at its full listed price, with discounts and fees appearing as discrete, separately reconcilable line items.

The immediate trigger for this work was a reconciliation issue where cart-level discount codes were collapsing invoice totals — showing only the net amount paid rather than the full product price plus discount — which broke QuickBooks product-reporting for accounting partners.

2. Guiding Principles

IDPrincipleRationale
P1Full product prices are always preserved on the invoice. Discounts and fees are never folded into the product price.Required for accurate QuickBooks product-level revenue reporting.
P2Every discount and every fee appears as its own distinct line item on both the ExpoGenie invoice and the QuickBooks record.Dominica's reconciliation workflow depends on line-level visibility.
P3Discounts carry a negative sign (−). Fees carry a positive sign (+). Always.Consistent sign convention for accounting. No ambiguity.
P4When a discount and a fee co-exist, the fee is calculated on the post-discount subtotal — the amount the customer is actually being charged.A credit card processing fee should reflect what is being charged to the card, not the gross pre-discount amount.
P5A product-level discount cannot reduce that product's line item below $0.00. A cart-level discount cannot exceed the cart subtotal.Prevents negative invoice totals and accounting anomalies.
P6Every ExpoGenie line item must map to exactly one Product/Service in QuickBooks before the sync occurs.Orphaned line items cause QB sync failures.
P7All percentage-based calculations are rounded to 2 decimal places using standard half-up rounding before being pushed to QuickBooks.Prevents fractional-cent amounts that QuickBooks cannot process cleanly.

3. Discount Scenarios

Discount codes are created by the event organizer and applied by the exhibitor at checkout. One code per order. Four supported permutations based on scope (product vs. cart) and type (fixed dollar vs. percentage). In all cases the discount is a negative line item — never a reduction in the listed product price.

4. Fee Scenarios

Fees are automatically applied by ExpoGenie based on conditions defined by the event organizer — such as payment method, product type, or registration timing. Unlike discount codes (which the exhibitor applies), fees are system-triggered. One fee maximum per order in v1. Fees are always positive amounts that increase the total owed and are never folded into the product price.

6. Edge Cases & Business Rules

E1

Discount Amount Exceeds Product Price (Product-Level Code)

Situation: An organizer creates a fixed product-level discount code for $300 off the Booth Staff Pass, which is listed at $200. The discount would put the line item below $0.

Rule: ExpoGenie validates this at discount code creation time and blocks the code from being saved. Error shown: "Discount amount ($300.00) cannot exceed the product list price ($200.00). Use a 100% percentage code to fully comp this product."

Validation should also re-run at checkout in case the product price changed after the code was created. If the product price has since dropped below the discount amount, the exhibitor sees a message that the code is invalid and is prompted to contact the organizer. (See Open Decision OD-04.)
E2

Discount Amount Exceeds Cart Total (Cart-Level Code)

Situation: A cart-level fixed discount code for $1,500 is applied to a cart totaling $1,200. The total would go negative.

Rule: ExpoGenie caps the discount at the cart subtotal. Invoice shows the discount equal to the subtotal, resulting in a $0.00 Total Due. Exhibitor sees: "Your discount has been applied. No payment is required for this order."

The QuickBooks record still shows all product lines at full list price with the capped discount line — preserving an accurate record of what was sold and fully comped.
E3

Full Comp — 100% Discount

Situation: An exhibitor is fully comped — a 100% discount code or a fixed code equal to the full cart total is applied.

Rule: Invoice shows all products at full list price with a discount line equal to the subtotal. Total Due = $0.00. No payment is collected.

Fee behavior: If a percentage cart fee would normally apply, it is suppressed when Total Due is $0.00 — nothing is being charged, so the fee base is zero.

The QB record still contains all product revenue lines and the offsetting discount line — preserving an accurate accounting record of what was sold, what it was worth, and that it was comped.
E4

Dynamic Recalculation — Discount Applied After Fee Was Already Displayed

Situation: An exhibitor is shown a CC processing fee based on the gross subtotal. They then enter a promo code, reducing the subtotal. The fee must recalculate.

Rule: Any time the cart changes (product added/removed, promo code applied/removed, payment method changed), the system recalculates the full sequence: (1) Sum products, (2) Apply discount, (3) Calculate fee on post-discount total. Fee amounts update dynamically. The exhibitor always sees the correct final total before confirming payment.

E5

Percentage Rounding

Situation: A percentage calculation produces a fractional cent (e.g., 10% of $1,233.33 = $123.333...).

Rule: All percentage-based discount and fee amounts are rounded to 2 decimal places using standard half-up rounding before display and before QB push. The rounded dollar amount is the value of record. QuickBooks receives only clean 2-decimal values.

If a discount or fee line item is pushed to QuickBooks before its corresponding QB Product/Service exists, the sync will fail and the transaction will not be recorded. ExpoGenie must surface a configuration warning during event setup if any required QB mapping is missing — before the event goes live.

8. Open Decisions

These items require explicit confirmation before E2E begins development. No development should proceed on the affected scenarios until these are resolved.

OD-01 · Dominica to Confirm

Product-Level Discount QB Mapping: Sub-Product vs. Shared Product

This document proposes that product-level discounts map to a unique QB product per product type (e.g., "Booth Staff Pass — Discount"). This gives clean per-product revenue and discount reporting in QB without manual reclassification. The alternative is a single shared "Promo Code Discount" product for all discount types (simpler QB setup, less granular reporting). Dominica needs to confirm which she prefers given her QB reporting workflows.

OD-02 · E2E + Derek to Confirm

Product-Level % Fee Calculation When a Product Discount Also Applies to the Same Item

Scenario F4 combined with D1 or D3 creates ambiguity: if a Booth Staff Pass is $200 with a $100 discount applied, and a 10% handling fee is on the pass — is the fee 10% of $200 (full list price) or 10% of $100 (post-discount price)? Principle P4 was written for cart-level fees. This needs an explicit decision before development.

OD-03 · Derek to Confirm

Credit Card Fee Trigger: At Checkout or Post-Payment?

For the credit card processing fee (F3, C1, C2): does the fee appear on the invoice at checkout when the exhibitor selects "Pay by Credit Card" — before payment is confirmed? Or only after payment clears? Showing it at checkout is better UX (no surprises) but requires the fee to be conditionally displayed based on payment method selection. Derek to confirm preferred behavior.

OD-04 · E2E to Confirm

Discount Validation: Creation Time vs. Checkout Time vs. Both

Edge Case E1 proposes validating product-level discount amounts against the product list price at code creation time. However, list prices can change after a code is created. Should validation also run at checkout? If yes, what does the exhibitor see when a previously valid code is now invalid? E2E to confirm feasibility and recommended handling.
